# Skill: Product Thinking

## Purpose
Ensure interface decisions serve a coherent product outcome rather than merely producing attractive screens.

## Procedure
1. Identify target user and context.
2. State the desired outcome plainly.
3. Identify the job/task.
4. Identify constraints: time, knowledge, device, trust, permissions, data, environment.
5. Separate essential workflow steps from optional capabilities.
6. Identify the highest-value action.
7. Remove/defer features that do not support the outcome.
8. Define success.
9. Design around the user's mental model and task sequence.
10. Check whether implementation supports the intended outcome.

## Questions
- Who is this for?
- What are they trying to accomplish?
- What must they know before acting?
- What decision are they making?
- What could make them hesitate?
- What could cause an expensive mistake?
- What information is essential versus merely available?
- What is the fastest safe path?

## Anti-patterns
Feature-first design, adding options because data exists, treating all requirements as equally important, optimizing screenshots instead of task completion, and copying competitors without understanding context.

## Output
User / Goal / Context / Primary task / Critical information / Primary action / Risks-friction / Product direction
